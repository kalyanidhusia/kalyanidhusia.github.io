Machine learning · data leakage

Your model scored 96 percent. Should anyone believe it?

A biomedical classifier is judged on data it has never seen. Leakage is any crack that lets test information reach training — and it quietly inflates accuracy. The classics: the same patient lands in both train and test (the model learns to recognize the person, not the disease); preprocessing is fit on all the data before splitting; features are selected using the whole dataset. Leaky pipelines ace cross-validation and collapse on the protected holdout — or worse, in the clinic.

After playing you can…
  • Split patient data so no patient (and, when the claim demands it, no hospital) straddles train and test.
  • Order a pipeline correctly: fit preprocessing and select features inside training data only.
  • Read a results table with suspicion: say why a sky-high cross-validation score can be meaningless, and which number to trust.
  1. 1Split phase: 10 patient visits arrive one by one — tap Train or Test for each. Watch the patient IDs: repeat visits must follow their patient, and at least 2 patients must be held out for testing.
  2. 2Pipeline phase: two setup decisions — where to fit the scaler, where to select features. One option is honest; the others leak.
  3. 3Reveal: your pipeline and the leaky one you avoided are both scored — cross-validation AND a protected holdout from a new hospital. First 2 wrong taps are practice; after that, 3 slips end the run.

The holdout is sacred: it gets looked at once, at the end, after every choice is frozen.

Simplified on purpose: the accuracy numbers are illustrative, not computed from a real model, and two hospitals stand in for the whole zoo of batch and site effects. Real leakage is also sneakier than three taps — temporal leakage, label proxies hiding inside features, and duplicated samples all count. The three cracks you sealed (grouped patients, fit-on-train-only, selection inside folds) are the true, most common ones — straight from the scikit-learn pitfalls page and a decade of retracted biomarker papers.
