BLASTP · heuristic local search

You are BLAST — too busy for the full matrix.

A protein database holds millions of sequences. Filling a Needleman–Wunsch table against every one would take forever, so BLASTP cheats, brilliantly: it chops the query into short words (3 amino acids), looks only where a word already matches — a seed — and extends outward from it while the score stays good. No seed? The sequence is skipped entirely, even if a decent alignment was hiding there. That is the trade: guaranteed-best is swapped for fast, and the E-value then says how many hits this good you'd expect by pure chance.

After playing you can…
  • Explain seeding: why matching a short word first makes search fast, and what happens to sequences with no seed.
  • Explain extension: grow the hit outward while the score holds, and stop when it decays — a local alignment.
  • Give the E-value idea — expected chance hits at this score — and name Smith–Waterman as the exact local counterpart.

  2. 2Extend the hit left and right: matches +2, mismatches −1. Watch the running score.
  3. 3Stop at the peak. Extending into junk bleeds points — stopping while the score is high is exactly BLAST's drop-off rule. 2 free practice taps, then 3 slips end the run.
