Innate immunity · phagocytosis & opsonization

You are a macrophage — the immune system's clean-up cell.

Phagocytes such as macrophages patrol your tissues and engulf pathogens and debris — a process called phagocytosis. Some bacteria have slippery capsules that resist engulfment. The counter-move is opsonization: antibodies coat the pathogen, turning it into an easy target. The power-up in this game is real immunology.

After playing you can…
  • Define phagocytosis and name the cells that do it.
  • Explain opsonization: antibodies tag pathogens so phagocytes engulf them.
  • Say why innate and adaptive immunity work as a team — and where vaccines fit in.
