Chromatin Command

Keep the heart's gene switchboard set right

You run the acetylation switch.

Heart cells tune gene activity with removable acetyl marks on histone tails. Writers (HATs) add marks — chromatin relaxes and the gene can be transcribed. Erasers (HDACs) remove marks — chromatin compacts and the gene goes quiet. Readers such as BRD4 dock on the marks and recruit RNA polymerase. The acetyl groups come from acetyl-CoA, a metabolite — so the cell's metabolism is wired directly into its gene regulation. In the stressed or diabetic heart this switchboard is mis-set, which is why HDAC inhibitors like SAHA (vorinostat) are studied as therapies — and why they're a double-edged sword: blocking the erasers de-represses everything they were holding shut.

Your mission

  • YOU are the HAT cannon. Shoot acetyl marks (costs acetyl-CoA) to flip a gene gate OPEN. You can only open — you're a writer, not an eraser.
  • GREEN gates = protective adult genes (SERCA2a, MYH6, RYR2, PGC-1α, CPT1B). Keep them OPEN so polymerase can transcribe them.
  • RED gates = fetal stress genes (NPPA, NPPB, MYH7). Keep them CLOSED — an open red gate leaks toxic stress transcripts.
  • HDAC ships erase marks, closing any gate they cross. Annoying on green gates — but they're the only thing that can close a red gate you opened by mistake. Erasers are regulators, not villains.
  • Polymerase ships dive at gates. Open green: transcript scored, heart healed. Closed green: stalled, heart damaged. Open red: toxic transcript, heart damaged. Closed red: stress gene silenced, bonus.
  • SAHA blockade freezes every HDAC and locks all gates for a few seconds. Ultimate defense for your green gates — but any red gate open at that moment is stuck open, leaking. Eraser inhibition is not gene-specific.

Named simplifications — what the game fakes

  • Real chromatin is 3D, with dozens of modifiable residues and locus-specific targeting — not eight binary gates in a row.
  • HDACs come in four classes with different (often non-histone) substrates; SAHA inhibits several continuously — it doesn't "freeze ships" for five seconds, and no drug hits all HDACs equally.
  • "Protective vs. stress" is a teaching split of the adult vs. fetal gene program; real gene regulation is context-dependent.
  • Polymerase doesn't dive-bomb genes: readers (e.g., BRD4) and transcription factors recruit it. "Open = transcribable" is the modeled truth.
  • The true part: acetylation is reversible; writer/eraser balance sets gene output; the marks are drawn from acetyl-CoA, tying metabolism to gene control; and inhibiting the erasers de-represses genes indiscriminately — the exact double-edge of HDAC therapeutics.
