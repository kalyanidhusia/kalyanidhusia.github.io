HAT-a-Mole

The acetylation switch, played as reflex

Same switchboard, whack-a-mole rules.

Heart cells tune gene activity with removable acetyl marks on histone tails. Writers (HATs) add marks — chromatin relaxes and the gene can be transcribed. Erasers (HDACs) remove marks — the gene goes quiet. Readers such as BRD4 dock on the marks and recruit RNA polymerase, and the acetyl groups come from acetyl-CoA, wiring metabolism into gene regulation. HDAC inhibitors like SAHA are studied as cardiometabolic therapies — and are double-edged, because blocking the erasers de-represses everything.

♥ Same concept as Chromatin Command and Cardio Beat — three games, one switchboard. Here the lesson lives in the erasers: sometimes the HDAC popping up is the only thing saving you.

Your mission

  • Nucleosomes pop up from eight chromatin loci. GREEN = protective genes (SERCA2a, MYH6, RYR2, PGC-1α…) — tap to acetylate before they sink back.
  • RED = fetal stress genes (NPPA, NPPB, MYH7) — leave them alone. Tap one by mistake and you've written a mark on a stress gene: it stays marked, and the next sweep will transcribe it.
  • HDAC erasers pop up on MARKED loci and steal the mark if you let them finish. On a green mark that's a loss — tap the HDAC to bop it. On a red mark you mis-wrote, the eraser is your only rescue — let it work.
  • The polymerase sweep crosses the board on a timer and transcribes whatever is marked: green marks score and heal; red marks leak toxic transcripts. Survive 8 sweeps.
  • SAHA stopwatch: freezes every eraser (and new pop-ups) until the sweep. Your green marks are untouchable — and so is any red mark you were hoping an HDAC would clean up. First 2 damaging mistakes are free practice.

Controls

Tap or click the loci directly · SPACE or the button = SAHA stopwatch.

After playing you can…

  1. Name the writer, eraser, and reader of acetylation (HAT, HDAC, BRD4) and what each does.
  2. State what acetylation does to chromatin and transcription.
  3. Explain why HDACs are regulators, not villains — including when an eraser is the rescue.
  4. Explain why an HDAC inhibitor raises expression of helpful and harmful genes.

Named simplifications — what the game fakes

  • Eight pop-up loci stand in for genome-wide chromatin; genes don't surface and sink — accessibility shifts are gradual.
  • Real HATs don't choose targets by reflex: transcription factors and readers recruit them to loci. The tap is the teaching version of "a writer acted here."
  • The polymerase sweep is a metronome for "transcription samples whatever is open right now" — real initiation is continuous and gene-specific.
  • SAHA doesn't freeze erasers in five-second pulses; inhibition is continuous and incomplete across HDAC classes.
  • The true part: writers mark, erasers unmark, marked = transcribable; a mark written on the wrong gene is only removed by an eraser — HDACs are regulators, and inhibiting them (the stopwatch) protects your good marks AND locks in your mistakes. That is the drug's double edge.
