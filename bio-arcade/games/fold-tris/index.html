Protein folding · the hydrophobic effect

A protein folds to hide its oily parts from water.

Proteins are chains of amino acids. Some residues are hydrophobic (H) — oily, water-fearing — and some are polar (P) — water-loving. In the cell's watery interior, the chain folds so H residues bury into the core and P residues face the water. That buried core is what makes a protein stable and functional.

After playing you can…
  • Explain what drives protein folding: hydrophobic residues burying away from water.
  • Predict where a residue belongs: H inside the core, P on the surface.
  • Connect misfolding and aggregation to real diseases, and say what chaperones do.
