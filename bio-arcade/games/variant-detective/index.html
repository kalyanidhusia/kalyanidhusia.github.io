Variant calling · evidence, not certainty

You are the variant reviewer — the pileup is your crime scene.

Align millions of reads to a reference genome and mismatches appear everywhere. Most are not mutations: sequencers miscall bases, reads land in the wrong place, certain sequence contexts breed systematic errors. A variant caller weighs evidence — base quality (how sure the machine was of each letter), mapping quality (how sure the aligner was of the read's position), strand balance (real variants show up on both DNA strands), and sequence context (homopolymer runs like AAAAA cause slips). Your job: for each candidate site, decide real variant, artifact, or — when the data cannot support either call — unresolved.

After playing you can…
  • Name the four evidence types and say what each one rules in or out.
  • Explain why two sites with the SAME alt-read count can deserve opposite verdicts.
  • Say why 'the variant is real' never by itself means 'the variant causes disease.'
  1. 1Each case shows a read pileup with one highlighted column. You get an evidence budget of 2 — tap at most two checks (base quality, mapping, strands, context) and read what they reveal.
  2. 2Then give a verdict: Real / Artifact / Unresolved. A correct verdict scores; a wrong one is a slip, and all four evidence lines are revealed so you see what you missed.
  3. 3Unresolved is a real answer. Calling a coin-flip 'real' is how false findings enter papers. First wrong verdict is free practice; after that, 3 slips end the shift.

Simplified on purpose: real callers do not spend a 2-check budget — they integrate ALL evidence probabilistically, converting per-base and per-read qualities into genotype likelihoods, and hard cases get settled by orthogonal validation (a second technology), not by a verdict button. The evidence types, the artifact signatures, and 'unresolved is an answer' are the true parts; the budget exists to make you choose what discriminates.
